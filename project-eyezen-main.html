"What if we could intuitively feel that we're too close to the screen without any notification at all?"

An eye-care software that uses the front camera to detect viewing distance and applies corresponding levels of screen blur — replacing intrusive alerts and naturally guiding users to correct their posture without disrupting workflow.

2024/02

Role User research, Interaction design, Prototyping
Team 1 Computer Science student, 2 Industrial Design students

context

We rarely notice how close we've gotten to the screen until our eyes start to ache.

Deep focus at a computer causes us to unconsciously drift closer and closer to the screen — a direct contributor to worsening nearsightedness and neck pain, yet it is something people rarely notice in the moment.

research

Why is it so hard to keep your distance from the screen? And why do existing solutions fail?

We ran semi-structured interviews and a short diary study to learn when people notice eye strain, how they currently cope (breaks, brightness, distance), and what would feel acceptable from software that uses the camera. Findings informed blur thresholds and how aggressively the effect should ramp with distance.
